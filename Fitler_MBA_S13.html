Pick your entity

Predict before you walk the table: "Two of you start working together tomorrow and file nothing. What are you, legally?" A general partnership. Each of you is personally on the hook for what the other does.

Two things people get wrong

Predict before the click: "You formed an LLC. Name two ways you can still lose your house."

Story slot: a veil that didn't hold.

Fitler MBA · S13 · Part 4

Employment law: hire an expert

This is the one area where you don't improvise. Walk the six jobs.

Click: the case. Éclat-shaped and hypothetical. "1099 or W-2?" They're employees.

The test is control, and whether they're in business for themselves. The label in the contract doesn't decide it.

The federal test is moving again. The Labor Department proposed a new rule in February 2026. The IRS and Pennsylvania apply their own tests.

Wrong call: back taxes, overtime, penalties.

Salaried is not the same as exempt from overtime.

Handbook: at-will language, and only policies you'll actually follow.

Files: write it down the day it happens. The file is the defense.

Firing: no surprises, consistent, paid out correctly. Severance in exchange for a signed release.

A remote hire in another state makes you an employer there. Federal, state and Philadelphia each add rules.

Fitler MBA · S13 · Part 5

Four kinds of IP

Four rows, four different tools. Most of this room lives in the first three.

Click: the case. Éclat-shaped and hypothetical. "Patent or trade secret?"

Trade secret. A patent publishes the recipe and then expires. Patent when it can be reverse-engineered and the cost is worth it. Secret when you can really keep it: need-to-know, NDAs, locked files.

Trademark: search before you name anything. Registering the LLC or buying the domain gives you no trademark rights. Protection is country by country.

Patents, one line for this room: if you might have one, see a patent lawyer before you show it publicly, and pay the specialist to write the first filing. The depth is slide 26, after 7:30.
