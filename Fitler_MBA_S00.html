Why business school exists
Origins

Same moment.
New century.

1881
Joseph Wharton founds the first collegiate business school. The economy is moving from agrarian to industrial — and the country needs a new kind of leader. Stable systems require execution. New systems require search.
2026
The economy is moving from industrial knowledge work to AI-augmented everything. Yesterday's playbook was built for executing known business models. The work in front of us is search, not execution.

The Fitler MBA is built for this moment.

Not "learn what we already know." Not a degree to add to your wall. A practitioner's program for searching faster than anyone else in your market — with AI in the room every session.

If Wharton was the answer to 1881, this is part of the answer to 2026.

Prompt engineering
The structure that beats every other tip

Write a brief, not a wish.

Treat every prompt as the brief you'd give a sharp new analyst on day one. Five blocks. In any order. Memorize this.

Letter
Element
What to include
R
Role
Who is the AI being right now? "You are a contract lawyer." "You are Steve Blank coaching a founder."
T
Task
The specific action. Affirmative ("do this"), not negative ("don't do that"). Specific beats clever.
C
Context
Background, audience, constraints, source material. The brief you'd give a new analyst.
E
Examples
Three to five examples of input → desired output. Claude reads examples literally — they steer the answer.
F
Format
Exact output shape. Numbered list? JSON? 200 words? Don't make the model guess.
Memorize. Then internalize.12 / 20
FFitler MBA
Two prompts that change everything
Add these to the end of any RTCEF prompt

Two power moves.

01

Move I — Make it interview you

"Before you respond, ask me any questions that would help you produce a better answer."

Forces the model to surface its assumptions. You'll get questions back that reveal gaps in your own thinking. Use it before any high-stakes output.

02

Move II — Make it grade itself

"Now be a critical reviewer and rate that response 1–10. Iterate until it scores a 9.5 or better."

Self-critique trades a few seconds of compute for substantially better output. Models are harsher critics than they are first-pass writers.

Try both tonight on your own work13 / 20
FFitler MBA
What the framework actually does
Side by side

A wish vs. a brief.

Same task. Two prompts. The difference in output is enormous — and it's not because the model got smarter between them.

✗ Wish
"Help me write a marketing email for my new product."
What you get: a generic email anyone could have written. Vague headline. Polite CTA. Smells like AI. Goes to spam.
✓ Brief (RTCEF)
RYou're a direct-response copywriter in the style of Joanna Wiebe.
TWrite a 120-word email announcing our launch.
CAudience: founders who already follow me on LinkedIn. They know the problem.
ESubject line must feel like a text from a friend, not a press release.
FOne CTA. No emojis. Plain text. Reply-to-me energy.
What you get: a punchy email with a specific voice, audience awareness, and a single clear ask. Reads human. Earns the click.
