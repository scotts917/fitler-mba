Story · Promise · Change

5 · The bridge

This is where you say what marketing is for. Not ads. Not followers. Telling the story of the change you're going to make, to the people it's for, so they trust you enough to take the next step.

Story cue: the campaign that went nowhere. Money went out, nothing came back, and the reason was that the story was about the product, not the customer. Yours or a company you advised.

Then the MLSI line. Ask the room: what's in that sentence? A story (you were a competitor), a promise (you can be again), a change (you're not the person who stopped). Nothing about the pool.

The bar isn't a revenue line. It's where the tribe forms.

The story that resonates is the one that tells them who they are

6 · People like us

Give this one room. It's the heart of the lecture.

Godin's point: people don't buy a product, they buy a version of themselves. The job of the story is to tell them who they are and who they're with. "People like us do things like this" is the test for every piece of marketing. If a serious athlete reads it and doesn't see herself, it fails.

The bar. Nobody picks a training facility for the light fare. But the person who stays for a beer after league comes back next Tuesday, and brings a friend. Belonging is the retention plan.

Ask: who's the "us" in your business? Take two answers. If someone says "small business owners," push: which ones? What do they do that others don't?

8 · Where the buyer is

Three minutes. The stages are from S08. Don't re-teach them. The one idea here: the same buyer needs a different message at each stage, and most small businesses only ever talk to stage 1.

9 · Tribe or thing

Five minutes. The slide the room will argue about, so give it the most time of the three.

Red Bull sells energy drinks and never shows you one. Nike's "Just Do It" ads don't have a shoe in them. That's tribe. Then Nike runs a shoe ad with the weight and the drop and the price. Disney sells you a specific park on specific dates. That's the thing.

Same building at MLSI. The adult athlete is buying the tribe: who she gets to be. The parent is buying the thing: a program, numbers, a video for the recruiter. Same coach, opposite message.

The punchline is Nike on both sides. It's not brand companies versus product companies. It's a decision for each piece of marketing you put out, and most people never make it on purpose.

Participation beat. Thirty seconds. Their last email, post or ad. Which side? Take three answers.

Why small

A small test with a clear message tells you something. A big campaign with a vague one tells you nothing, and costs more.

One message · One channel · A number that means yes or no

11 · Smallest market

This ties back to slide 6. The smallest market is the tribe. You already know where they are.

Story cue: how your first ten customers actually showed up. Jibby, a WEMBA company, Vanguard online. Walk the route step by step. The point is that it was small, specific and measurable, and it told you what to do next.

The MLSI test is a real plan someone could run Monday. One message, one channel, a budget, a number. If ten people book, the message works and you buy the next ring. If two people book, don't buy a bigger list. Fix the message.
